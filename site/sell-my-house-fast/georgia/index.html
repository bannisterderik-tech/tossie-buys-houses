How foreclosure actually works in Georgia

Almost every Georgia foreclosure is non-judicial. No judge signs off, and the sale happens on the courthouse steps on the first Tuesday of the month. From the first missed payment to a completed auction can run as little as 60 days.

Right of redemption: There is no right of redemption after a non-judicial foreclosure sale in Georgia. The 12-month redemption people hear about applies only to judicial foreclosures, which are rare here.

Because there is no redemption after the gavel, the sale date is the real deadline. Once it passes, the house is gone.

Delinquent property taxes in Georgia

Georgia tax sales are separate from mortgage foreclosure and run on a different clock. A Georgia tax sale carries a 12-month right of redemption running from the sale date. You can reimburse the purchaser what they paid plus statutory premium and reclaim the property.

Closing in Georgia

Georgia real estate closings are conducted under the supervision of a licensed Georgia attorney, not a title agent working alone. That matters for timing: the attorney runs the title search and the disbursement, so scheduling follows their calendar.

Sources: Georgia Foreclosure Laws (Nolo) · Right of Redemption, Athens-Clarke County. This is general information about Georgia law, not legal advice.
