What North Carolina law means for New Hanover County owners

North Carolina sits between judicial and non-judicial. A power of sale foreclosure cannot proceed until the Clerk of Superior Court in the property's county holds a hearing and authorizes it. The procedure is in Chapter 45, Article 2A of the General Statutes. The clerk's hearing adds a scheduled, public step that Georgia and Tennessee do not have.

There is no post-sale redemption period. Instead, a 10-day upset bid window runs after the sale, and every new upset bid restarts a fresh 10-day clock.

North Carolina counties foreclose on tax liens through the courts rather than selling lien certificates. An owner can pay off the taxes and costs any time before the court confirms the sale.
